THE FILTER/SEARCH VISION

The internet should work for us.

I want to build tools that help us accomplish our goals online, rather than constantly pulling us toward someone else's. More control over what we see, what we ignore, and how we spend our attention.

What it could do

Your attention. Your rules.

These are ideas I'd love to explore as Filter/Search grows. Not promises about what exists today, but possibilities for a more user-controlled web.

📢

Ad transparency

Count how many ads appear on Facebook, LinkedIn, and other platforms. See what percentage of your feed is advertising instead of having to guess.

🤖

AI-content transparency

Estimate how much of a feed may be AI-generated, with confidence levels and honest uncertainty when the origin of a post can't be reliably determined.

🎯

Intent-based browsing

Choose what you're here to do. Catch up with family, find a job, or learn something new—without getting pulled away from that goal at every turn.

🚫

Less engagement bait

Filter the patterns you find distracting: rage bait, engagement farming, endless recommendations, thirst traps, or content from pages you never chose to follow.

👥

People over recommendations

Prioritize people and pages you deliberately follow over recommendations selected because an algorithm predicts you'll keep scrolling.

⏳

Your time, your rules

Add limits and intentional friction around habits you want to manage. Opening a dating app shouldn't have to mean getting trapped in an endless swiping loop.

🔍

Explain the feed

Help distinguish followed accounts, paid promotions, and recommendations—and explain available signals about why something appeared.

⚙️

Rules you own

Make filters inspectable, configurable, and portable. Your preferences should be yours to change, export, and carry forward—not locked into one platform.

💬

Conversation beyond the platform

Imagine an opt-in layer for talking with other people about public pages, posts, and ideas—even when the original platform doesn't offer the kind of conversation you're looking for.

The bigger vision

A different kind of search engine.

Imagine something like a user-controlled Google for the social web. Configure what matters to you once, then use those preferences to shape your experience across supported sites.

But don't lose the unexpected discoveries. Sometimes you want to step outside your rules and see what the platform has to offer.

Why build this?

The web should serve the people using it.

Social platforms have their own goals. Users have theirs. Those goals can overlap, but they aren't always the same. Tools that help people express their intentions can make that difference visible—and give users more control over their time.

Agency over defaults

Make it easier to choose what belongs in your experience.

Transparency over mystery

Measure what can be measured. Explain what remains uncertain.

Discovery by choice

Keep room for surprise without making endless scrolling the default.

Our attention is part of our lives. The software we use should help us spend it the way we actually want to.
